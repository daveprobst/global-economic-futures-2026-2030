05 / EUROPE

Sequence matters

Europe does not win the crash. It may win the reallocation.

A US and China shock first hits exports, banks, dollar funding and demand. Relative gains arrive later, and only if Europe turns cheaper assets, compute and talent into capacity.

Acute phase · loses

Trade and credit contract; bank and market losses tighten finance.

Recovery phase · conditional upside

Lower direct AI concentration, inward capital, CRMA projects and cheaper compute can lift relative performance.

What must go right

  • Bank losses stay contained and dollar swap lines work.
  • Permitting and capital markets turn inflows into investment.
  • CRMA extraction, refining and recycling reach scale.
  • Energy costs and fragmentation do not erase the advantage.

12 / MODEL RISK

Calibration

Out-of-sample skill is modest and measured.

Baseline constraint · independent of crash odds

Weak trend growth is already the counterfactual.

The World Bank’s 2023 potential-growth assessment put 2022–2030 global potential growth near 2.2% a year—about one-third below the early-2000s pace—and warned that recession or financial crisis would reduce it further. The simulator uses this evidence to limit recovery ceilings; it does not use the warning to raise crash probabilities.
