Global Economic Futures, 2026–2030

Does AI infrastructure spending colliding with higher rates turn an AI scarcity boom into a real-economy correction? This explorer tests that thesis against sourced evidence and conditional simulations. It does not assume the outcome. Scenarios are paths to examine, not forecasts.

Overview and how to read this

The brief tests the thesis that AI capex competing with sustained rates above 5% produces a global liquidity collapse in 2026–2030, with the US and China breaking and the EU and Switzerland as relative anchors. The accepted evidence does not confirm that chain. It establishes which links are measurable, which are not, and what the simulations do and do not show.

Every number carries one of five labels. Read them before drawing conclusions.

Observed
Taken from a dated primary source (filing, central bank, statistics office, official or company project source). Source ID and date are shown.
Computed
Arithmetic on observed values, such as a percentage change from two published index levels. The inputs are cited.
Assumed
A design input chosen by the modeller within a stated range, such as a coupon, an AI-loss share or a feedback coefficient. Not an observation.
Simulated
An output of the accepted model runs (seed 20261014). Percentiles are conditional on the design assumptions, not calibrated probabilities.
Uncertain
The claim is supported only in part, is contested by counter-evidence, or rests on a single or secondary source. The ledger records the counter-evidence.

Stress tests, not probabilities. The 70% AI revenue-loss path is a stress test. Nothing on this site assigns it a probability. Percentile bands show spread across the assumed ranges, not the chance that an outcome occurs.

Executive findings

Of 66 claims in the five regional ledgers, 52 are supported, 14 are uncertain and 0 are contradicted. Each finding cites its claim IDs so you can open the evidence in the search below.

  1. The thesis's central comparison cannot yet be checked at issuer scope.

    Filings do not give an AI-only capex series or a comparable AI revenue series for the five firms. The working comparison of USD 150–200 bn capex against USD 40 bn AI revenue is therefore not verified, and consolidated capex cannot be assigned wholly to AI. Uncertain us_ai_capex_revenue_scope.

  2. The five operators are cash generative on a consolidated basis, but the cushions differ.

    Consolidated cash and operating cash flow are large. Over the latest periods, however, Amazon's trailing operating cash flow (USD 161.4 bn) was below its net property purchases (USD 169.0 bn) to 30 June 2026. Oracle's FY2026 cash capex (USD 55.7 bn) exceeded its operating cash flow (USD 32.0 bn). Observed Computed us_cloud_cash_generates, us_current_five_company_liquidity_not_uniform.

  3. Willingness to pay for AI is not yet measured.

    Microsoft reports more than 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats at the fiscal year ended 30 June 2026. These are adoption indicators. They do not give retention, net price, margins or elasticity. Observed us_paid_seats_are_adoption_not_elasticity.

  4. Global transmission is small in the accepted model.

    Under the severe combined design, the 2030 demand-index deviation has conditional median values of -0.68% for the US, -2.79% for China and -0.23% for EU27. The US p05–p95 band is -0.72% to -0.58%. These are deviations of a 2026=100 index. The result depends on the feedback matrices and the copula design, and most of the EU27 response comes from the generic feedback matrix (see model risk). Simulated global module, severe_combined_stress, 2030.

  5. The severe design does not make Switzerland a relative anchor.

    In the deterministic severe design, the global module's Switzerland adapter reaches -1.56% in 2030, against -0.24% for EU27 and -0.70% for the US. The anchor hypothesis is therefore not supported by this design. This is an output of the global regional adapter, not a Swiss macro forecast, and the Swiss branch model adds no GDP delta in its severe deterministic path. Simulated global module, severe_combined_stress, 2030.

  6. Global bank credit was not contracting at the end of Q1 2026.

    BIS measured cross-border bank credit of USD 39.5 tn at end-Q1 2026, up 11% year on year. This contradicts the proposition that the measured bank-credit stock was already contracting. Funding stress can still arise through US-dollar funding, local credit tightening and market depth. Observed global-bank-credit-not-contracting-q1-2026, BIS_GLI_2026Q1.

  7. Company simulations are withheld; the observed bridge stands on its own.

    The accepted company run omits refinancing proceeds from closing liquidity, and the corrected code has not been re-accepted. This site therefore publishes no simulated company liquidity paths. It publishes the observed issuer bridge and the gross commitment inventory (see Companies). Observed us_oracle_observation_vintages_separate.

  8. Refinancing pressure is lagged and firm-specific.

    Meta's full-note effective interest-rate range for its debt at 30 June 2026 is 3.63%–6.48%. The 10–12% refinancing coupon used in the prolonged-rate and severe paths is a stress assumption, not an observed market rate. Observed us_meta_note8_effective_rate_full_range. Assumed the 10–12% coupon range.

  9. Telecom 2001 is a cautionary analogue with clear limits.

    WorldCom, Level 3 and Verizon show that overbuild, debt service and restructuring can coincide. Their distress paths differ: WorldCom's was driven by restatement and governance failures, and Verizon was a regulated incumbent. No utilization denominator or realized salvage price exists for AI capacity, so telecom impairment data cannot calibrate AI asset-recovery rates. Observed telecom_analogue_limited.

  10. China: external and domestic funding must be separated.

    SAFE measured USD 2.498 tn of gross external debt at end-June 2026, 59% of it short-term on original maturity. Property investment fell 19.9% y/y over January–August 2026, and developer domestic-loan funding fell 33.3%. Japan's balance-sheet recession is a qualitative analogue, not evidence of a Japan-like path. Observed CN-EXTDEBT-01, CN-PROPERTY-01, CN-JAPAN-01.

  11. A single developer shows refinancing mismatch; it is not a sector estimate.

    China Vanke's reviewed but unaudited 2026 interim report, as held in a secondary text mirror, shows cash of RMB 53.1 bn against RMB 178.9 bn of principal due within one year. The independent review flags material going-concern uncertainty. The evidence is one issuer group, excluded from calibration. Uncertain CN-DEVELOPER-BS-01.

  12. Chinese deflation is not established at the economy-wide level.

    Headline CPI was +0.9% y/y in September 2026 and aggregate PPI was +5.0% y/y, while the living-materials PPI subcomponent was −0.1%. The evidence supports weak domestic demand, not a generalized household and healthy-firm deleveraging. Uncertain CN-DEMAND-01.

  13. The ~30% EU magnet-recycling claim is not supported.

    The accepted cohort and mass-balance model gives 8–19% secondary supply in 2030 for selected-application NdFeB demand (2.88–6.84 kt of 36 kt). The theoretical end-of-life feed of about 10 kt is 28% of 2030 demand before chain losses, which caps the arithmetic even before losses. Solvay's 30% figure is a company objective for 2030, not a reported achievement. Simulated Computed eu_recycling_not_exact_30pct, recycling_30pct_sufficiency_unsubstantiated, solvay_30pct_is_a_company_goal_not_recycling_observation.

  14. The alleged March 2026 EU hybrid automobile deal is not verified as enacted.

    No verified March 2026 EU-wide hybrid agreement was found in the primary legal record as of 14 October 2026. Regulation (EU) 2023/851 remains the enacted baseline. COM(2025) 995 proposes a 90% rather than 100% 2035 fleet reduction, and the ordinary legislative procedure is not complete. Observed automotive_hybrid_deal_misstates_status.

  15. EU banks report strong capital; that does not measure US or China exposure.

    The ECB's significant-institution subset reports a Q2 2026 CET1 ratio of 16.00%, and the EBA EU/EEA dashboard reports 16.1%. The two perimeters differ, and neither identifies country-level US or China credit. Observed eu_bank_resilience_perimeter_2026q2.

  16. The Swiss 0.6% inflation hypothesis is uncertain, not refuted.

    SECO's forecast gives 0.6% average inflation for both 2026 and 2027. The SNB's later assessment projects 0.7% and 0.8%. Observed CPI was +1.0% y/y in September 2026. Import prices (index 102.6, December 2025 = 100) rose 0.8% y/y in August 2026. Within that, other organic basic chemicals rose 8.0% y/y, and 12.8% in the single month of August. The September import-price release is scheduled for 15 October, after the cut-off. Observed Computed SWISS_INFLATION_FORECAST_06, CH_CPI_202609, FSO_IMPORT_PRICE_AUG2026.

  17. The Switzerland–EU package is signed, not in force.

    The package agreements were signed on 2 March 2026 and dispatched to Parliament on 13 March 2026. Signature and dispatch do not establish ratification or entry into force. Swiss participation in Horizon Europe follows a separate programmes agreement, signed in November 2025 and provisionally applied from 1 January 2025. Observed EU_PACKAGE_SIGNED_NOT_IN_FORCE, HORIZON_PROGRAMME_ACCESS_DISTINCT_FROM_PACKAGE.

  18. CHF safe-haven behaviour is bilateral; the 2015 export test found no significant aggregate decline.

    The CHF rises against some currencies in risk episodes and not others. In the 2015 appreciation, real goods export quantities showed no statistically significant aggregate decline, while sector responses differed. Merchanting sales (Q2 2026 gross turnover CHF 238.2 bn) are gross turnover, not value added, and should not be read as GDP. Observed CHF_SAFE_HAVEN_BILATERAL, SWISS_2015_EXPORT_QUANTITY_RESILIENCE, SWISS_MERCHANTING_GROSS_TURNOVER.

  19. Partial bank data should not be read as EU totals.

    BIS data show EU-reporter claims on US and China counterparties, but the coverage is partial. The accepted bounds report USD 2,281 bn of immediate-counterparty US claims across 12 EU27 reporters and USD 69.8 bn of China claims across 9 reporters. Neither is an EU total. Observed eu_crossborder_bank_claims_us_china_partial.

Scenarios, fan charts and regional comparison

Nine global scenario paths cover the base case, AI correction, prolonged high rates, a 70% AI revenue-loss stress, severe combined stress, Europe variants and Swiss branches. The fan chart shows p01–p99 and p05–p95 bands of the normalized demand-index deviation, from 10,000 conditional design draws (seed 20261014). The line is the deterministic path. Values are deviations of a 2026=100 index.

Stress path: the 70% AI revenue-loss scenario moves the US by -0.38% at the 2030 median and the other regions by about zero. It is a stress test with no assigned probability.

Reading the scenarios

The deterministic path and the conditional median differ where the draws move feedback coefficients and shared factors. Because global effects are small, the regional and sector modules carry most of the decision-relevant detail. Scenario labels describe conditions to test, not likelihoods.

Sensitivity and robustness

The browser only re-displays precomputed sweep outputs; no model runs in the page. Choose a parameter and region to see the 2030 deviation at each swept value, for the three stress scenarios that were swept. Copula dependence sweeps are in the downloads (global/global_dependence_sensitivity.csv).

Accounting: 49 identities, all passed. Global tests: 50 of 50 passed. Batch standard errors for each percentile are in the Monte Carlo summary (p05BatchStandardError).

What the observed figures do not show

  • No AI-only capex or AI revenue series is disclosed by these issuers, so an AI-specific liquidity bridge cannot be observed (us_ai_capex_revenue_scope).
  • Oracle's latest cash, debt and lease balances are dated 31 August 2026, while its published principal maturity ladder is dated 31 May 2026. The bridge keeps those vintages separate (us_oracle_observation_vintages_separate).
  • Utilization is not disclosed in comparable form, so no utilization-adjusted asset recovery can be inferred (us_utilization_not_comparable_publicly).

China: funding, property and demographics

The model links external and domestic funding, LGFV and property balance sheets, consumption and prices, and the working-age population. The channels are kept separate because the evidence separates them. Transmission is an assumption-based mapping, not a causal estimate.

Observed balance-sheet and funding facts

  • Observed SAFE: end-2025 external financial assets USD 11.786 tn, external liabilities USD 7.715 tn, net external assets USD 4.071 tn CN-STOCK-01.
  • Observed SAFE: end-June 2026 gross external debt USD 2.498 tn, of which USD 1.469 tn (59%) short-term on original maturity. Original maturity is not remaining maturity, and 41% of the short-term amount is trade-related credit CN-EXTDEBT-01.
  • Observed IMF 2026 Article IV: augmented debt 126.6% of GDP in 2025, with an estimated LGFV component of 50.9%. The augmented perimeter is not comparable with official budget debt CN-LGFV-01, CN-LGFV-PERIMETER-01.
  • Observed NBS, January–August 2026: real-estate investment −19.9% y/y; new commercial building sales area −12.1%; developer domestic-loan funding −33.3%. These are flows, not measures of losses CN-PROPERTY-01.
  • Uncertain China Vanke, one developer group: reviewed but unaudited interim figures show cash RMB 53.1 bn, current borrowings RMB 167.2 bn and RMB 178.9 bn of principal due within one year, with material going-concern uncertainty flagged. The source is a secondary text mirror. This is excluded from calibration and is not a sector loss estimate CN-DEVELOPER-BS-01.
  • Observed Working-age population: 851.36 m aged 16–59 in the year-end 2025 communiqué, versus 869.87 m aged 15–59 in the later 1% survey. The age bins and vintages differ, so this is not a revision CN-DEMO-01.
  • Uncertain Deflation and weak domestic demand are a live question, not an established household-deleveraging path. September 2026 headline CPI was +0.9% y/y and aggregate PPI +5.0% y/y, while the living-materials PPI was −0.1% y/y CN-DEMAND-01.
  • Observed Household balance sheet: PBOC household loans RMB 83.363 tn and household deposits RMB 163.308 tn as of November 2025. The ratio is aggregate and is not household net worth CN-HOUSEHOLD-BS-01.

Causal limits: the model links Western risk aversion, portfolio outflows and capital controls to domestic outcomes through assumed coefficients. The SAFE flow mix describes starting exposure, not a test of risk aversion. Japan's post-bubble repair is an analogue for collateral and bank-transmission channels; it does not predict a Japan-like path (CN-JAPAN-01, uncertain).

EU: debt exposures and raw-material stock-flows

Two EU questions are separated: which sovereigns and banks are exposed, and whether the recycling and refining pipeline can meet stated targets. On the second, the stock-flow arithmetic is the test.

What the evidence supports

  • Observed Eurostat, Q1 2026 release (published 21 July 2026): euro-area government debt 88.9% of GDP. Member-state gross debt runs from 25.2% of GDP (Estonia) to 143.5% (Greece), with France, Italy and Belgium above 100% eu_fiscal_positions_diverge, EUROSTAT_GOV_DEBT_Q1_2026.
  • Observed ECB, Q2 2026: euro-area non-financial corporate debt 66.0% of GDP consolidated (135.6% non-consolidated); household debt 80.3% of disposable income eu_private_debt_latest_q2_2026.
  • Observed CRMA 2030 benchmarks are capacity ambitions, not realized output crma_benchmarks_are_not_realized_output.
  • Observed LKAB's Kiruna rare-earth figure is a mineral resource, not a reserve or current mine output kiruna_resource_not_current_mine_output.
  • Observed Narva, Estonia: the company's facility opening was announced on 22 September 2025. A later Neo source describes commercial magnet production starting in Narva, but its publication date is not recorded in the registry. Carester's Caremag is financed with commissioning planned for late 2026. Neither establishes audited annual output or utilization at the cut-off europe_rare_earth_refining_pipeline_is_conditional, NEO_NARVA_2025_OPENING, NEO_2026_NARVA_COMMERCIAL.

  • Observed Solvay's 30% European-demand objective for 2030 is a forward-looking company objective. The accepted claim records commercial production at La Rochelle from April 2025, but no achieved recycling share solvay_30pct_is_a_company_goal_not_recycling_observation.

Switzerland: CHF, inflation, exports and bilateral branches

Two branches are kept apart: status-quo erosion, where bilateral access and the electricity and research arrangements weaken over time, and the signed package, which is conditional on ratification and entry into force. Neither is presented as enacted.

Branch assumptions for 2030 (design inputs)

Assumed The package branch assumes GDP growth of 2.1% in 2030 against 1.0% under status-quo erosion, with market access +0.3 pp against −0.4 pp, friendshoring +0.2 pp against −0.2 pp, and an electricity-cost index of −0.1 against +0.1. These are design choices with an assumed effective year, not estimated causal effects. The package effect is conditional on entry into force, which has not occurred.

Model caveats specific to Switzerland

  • The deterministic severe branch has no CPI pass-through, and its GDP deltas are zero in the deterministic path. Its inflation result is therefore not a full macro outcome.
  • The FX anchors are hard-coded in the model. The separate Swiss specification folder is not consumed by the run, and it diverges from the run.
  • The Swiss branches are conditional. No causal estimate of bilateral CHF or market-access effects is made.

Comparability limits

  • WorldCom restatement means contemporaneous reported data was materially unreliable; use the later audited restated statements.
  • WorldCom’s acquisition, governance and fraud story differs from a pure infrastructure oversupply shock.
  • Level 3 was itself restructured and management forward estimates are not actual asset recoveries.
  • Verizon was a regulated/local-access and wireless incumbent with different demand, customer mix and asset network economics.
  • No common utilization denominator, per-route-mile capacity, network lease commitments, or realized salvage price was found in these disclosures. Do not infer AI data-center asset recovery rates from telecom impairment/book values.

Global Crossing appears in the accepted US mechanism and source records, but no accepted Global Crossing financial case is in the analogue set. It is therefore not compared numerically here.

    Model risk the reader should weigh

    • Global effects are small relative to the scenario labels. Conditional bands are wide relative to their centres and are not calibrated probabilities.
    • Feedback matrices, copula loading (0.5), the overlap bounds and the refinancing coupon are design assumptions.
    • The US intensity path is clipped at its 0.7 AI-loss bound.
    • The company simulation is withheld. Its accepted run omits refinancing proceeds, and the corrected code is not re-accepted.
    • The China and Swiss Monte Carlo summaries are design sensitivities over independent uniform bounds. They are not probability-calibrated, and the Swiss severe branch has no CPI pass-through.

    • tests receipt covers only global/company/china suites; EU and Swiss suites not in executor receipt (re-run manually: pass). Recommend adding to runner.
    • ~98% of severe 2030 EU27 response is from generic global feedback matrix; possible China->EU27 double counting; EU bank coefficient unestimated; safe-haven flows not quantified
    • US intensity clipped at 0.7 AI-loss bound
    • Swiss FX anchors hardcoded in model.py; models/swiss_spec non-consumed and divergent
    • Feedback matrices, copula loading, overlap A16 bounds, refinancing coupon are design assumptions; percentiles are conditional design percentiles, not calibrated probabilities or forecasts
    • Dependence/seed sweeps at 2000 draws
    • Receipt validator in repair was a Python port; this manifest is the executor's own output
