01 · HOW LIKELY?

Seven futures, one window

How likely is each outcome?

We sorted the possible futures into seven outcomes that add up to 100. Each is defined by what share prices do between now and the end of 2030, and by what happens to money markets and banks along the way. Odds are anchored in what happened after comparable booms, then adjusted for today’s funding conditions.

The small range under each number is a 10th–90th percentile band: with only nine independent historical waves to learn from, the honest answer is a range, not a point.

02 · THE UNITED STATES

Where the stress would start

A few giant companies carry most of the AI spending.

A handful of tech giants are spending hundreds of billions on chips, power and data centres, partly with borrowed money. If the revenue they expected from AI arrives later or smaller than hoped, share prices fall, lenders get more careful, and the spending slows. How much that matters for everyone else depends on whether funding markets keep working.

How big is it? Estimated

Roughly one dollar in ten of US company shares

04 · EUROPE

Relative position

Europe is hit less, and may come out with more standing.

Phase 1 · cushioned

Little direct AI exposure and no property-debt loop. The hit arrives second-hand: weaker exports, some bank losses, tighter dollar funding.

Phase 2 · a window of opportunity (if things go right)

With the US and China both weakened, Europe could draw money seeking safety and gain weight in world affairs. This part is a scenario judgment: the model does not simulate it.

What would have to go right

  • Safe-asset appeal. Euro-area government bonds and the euro must be deep and liquid enough to take in nervous money without straining local credit.
  • Independence from outside shocks. Energy and critical-materials supply keep diversifying. The EU’s 2030 targets are objectives, not capacity yet: 10% of its needs mined, 40% processed and 25% recycled at home, and no more than 65% from any single outside country.
  • Sound banks and working dollar lines. Bank losses stay contained and central-bank swap lines work.
  • Acting together, quickly. Governments use the window instead of arguing through it.

10 · LIMITS

What this page cannot tell you

Useful for thinking. Not a forecast, not advice.

    Baseline note

    Weak trend growth is already the starting point.

    The World Bank’s 2023 assessment put average global potential growth for 2022–2030 near 2.2% a year, about a third below the early-2000s pace. Potential growth is a long-run speed, not a ceiling: after a downturn the economy can temporarily grow faster than that, and the model allows it (the output gap closes at 30% a year in the US).
