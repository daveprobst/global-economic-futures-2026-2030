05 / EUROPE

Sequence matters

Europe does not win the crash. It may win the reallocation.

A US/China shock first hits exports, banks, dollar funding and confidence. Relative gains arrive later only if Europe turns cheaper assets, compute and talent into productive capacity.

Acute phase · loses

Trade and credit contract; bank and market losses tighten finance.

Recovery phase · conditional upside

Lower direct AI concentration, inward capital, CRMA projects and cheaper compute can improve relative performance.

What must go right

  • Bank losses stay contained and dollar swap lines work.
  • Permitting and capital markets convert inflows into investment.
  • CRMA extraction, refining and recycling reach scale.
  • Energy costs and fragmentation do not erase the advantage.

Severe branch: annual real GDP growth

The EU trough is shallower here, but still recessionary. Reallocation is delayed and conditional.

10 / MODEL RISK

Honest uncertainty

The model has signal.
It does not have certainty.

The crash model is selected on out-of-sample calibration. A second, separately validated transition model estimates when an equity loss becomes a banking crisis. Both retain wide uncertainty because global crash clusters are scarce.

Baseline constraint · independent of crash odds

Weak trend growth is already the counterfactual.

The World Bank’s 2023 potential-growth assessment put 2022–2030 global potential growth near 2.2% a year—about one-third below the early-2000s pace—and warned that recession or financial crisis would reduce it further. The simulator uses this evidence to limit recovery ceilings; it does not use the warning to increase crash probabilities.
